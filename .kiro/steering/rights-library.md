# Rights library (only these statements may be shown as rights)

Each entry becomes a record in src/lib/rights/library.json with id, plain_statement, applies_when, source_url.

1. shelter-no-exclusion: Emergency shelter programs must not exclude people with disabilities, and generally cannot require them to go to a special-needs or medical shelter instead of a regular shelter. Source: https://archive.ada.gov/pcatoolkit/chap7shelterprog.htm
2. shelter-service-animal: Shelters must change "no pets" rules so people are not separated from their service animals. Source: https://www.ada.gov/topics/emergency-planning
3. shelter-communication: Shelters run by state or local government must communicate effectively with people who are blind, deaf, or hard of hearing. Source: https://www.ada.gov/topics/emergency-planning
4. boston-ada-grievance: You can file an ADA grievance if the City of Boston discriminated against you because of a disability or did not give you access to meetings, services, programs, or activities. The Disabilities Commission contacts you within 30 days. Source: https://www.boston.gov/departments/disabilities-commission/how-file-ada-grievance-boston
5. boston-curb-ramp: Boston has an ADA Curb Ramp Request Form for corners where a curb ramp is missing or does not meet ADA rules. Contacting the Disabilities Commission can help accessibility-related 311 requests get followed up. Source: https://boston.gov/departments/youth-engagement-and-advancement/youth-guide-street-services
6. fema-accessibility-repairs: For disasters declared on or after March 22, 2024, FEMA can pay for accessibility items such as ramps, grab bars, and paved pathways for a disaster-damaged home, even if they were not there before, and this does not count against the housing assistance maximum. You may be eligible; FEMA decides. Source: https://www.fema.gov/fact-sheet/updated-fema-programs-better-assist-people-disabilities
7. fema-medical-equipment: FEMA Other Needs Assistance can help with disaster-caused medical costs, including lost medication and medical equipment. Source: https://www.fema.gov/fact-sheet/fema-assistance-other-needs
8. fema-late-registration: Since March 2024, people who apply after the 60-day window can give an explanation for the delay instead of supporting documents. Source: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12785.html
9. fema-helpline: Apply or ask questions by calling the FEMA helpline at 1-800-621-3362, or at DisasterAssistance.gov. Source: https://www.fema.gov/disaster/4780
10. fema-appeal: You have the right to appeal a FEMA decision; the decision letter states the deadline. Source: https://www.fema.gov/disaster/4780
